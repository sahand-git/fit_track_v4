## 2. Terminology & Definitions

- **Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)**: The average daily dietary nutrient intake level sufficient to meet the nutrient requirement of nearly all (97–98%) healthy individuals in a particular life-stage and gender group.
- **Adequate Intake (AI)**: A recommended average daily nutrient intake level based on observed or experimentally determined approximations or estimates of nutrient intake by a group (or groups) of apparently healthy people that are assumed to be adequate. Used when an RDA cannot be determined.
- **Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL)**: The highest level of daily nutrient intake that is likely to pose no risk of adverse health effects to almost all individuals in the general population. As intake increases above the UL, the potential risk of adverse effects may increase.
- **Chronic Disease Risk Reduction Intake (CDRR)**: An intake level above which intake reduction is expected to reduce chronic disease risk (established for Sodium in 2019).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Safety Rules Regarding Upper Limits (UL)**:
> 1. **Never use a Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) as an intake goal.** A UL is a toxicity threshold ceiling, not a target.
> 2. **Never compare a supplement-only limit with total food intake.** Certain nutrients (e.g. Magnesium, Niacin, Folate, Vitamin E) have ULs that apply strictly to pharmacological/supplemental/synthetic forms. Intake from natural whole foods does not count against these limits.

## 3. Adult Age & Gender Specific Targets (DRIs)

FitTrack targets are strictly formulated for **adults aged 19 and older**.

### Exclusions & Medical Scope
- **Users under 19 years of age**: Pediatric and adolescent nutritional requirements undergo rapid developmental shifts. FitTrack does not present adult RDA/AI targets to minors (<19).
- **Pregnancy & Lactation**: Nutritional requirements during pregnancy and lactation differ substantially from non-pregnant adult baselines and are **not handled**.
- **Pathological & Medical Conditions**: Individuals with chronic kidney disease, inborn errors of metabolism, hemochromatosis, malabsorption syndromes, or those undergoing specific pharmacotherapy require individualized clinical nutrition therapy outside the scope of this general lifestyle tracker.

## 4. Bio-Equivalence & Conversion Mathematics

### A. Vitamin A: Retinol Activity Equivalents (RAE)
The body converts provitamin A carotenoids to retinol with variable efficiency:
$$1\text{ mcg RAE} = 1\text{ mcg retinol} = 2\text{ mcg supplemental }\beta\text{-carotene} = 12\text{ mcg dietary }\beta\text{-carotene} = 24\text{ mcg dietary }\alpha\text{-carotene or }\beta\text{-cryptoxanthin}$$
- **From International Units (IU)**:
  - $1\text{ IU retinol} = 0.3\text{ mcg RAE}$
  - $1\text{ mcg RAE} = 3.33\text{ IU retinol}$
  - $1\text{ IU supplemental }\beta\text{-carotene} = 0.3\text{ mcg RAE}$
  - $1\text{ IU dietary }\beta\text{-carotene} = 0.05\text{ mcg RAE}$

### B. Vitamin D: Micrograms and International Units
Vitamin D biological activity is standardized across cholecalciferol (D3) and ergocalciferol (D2):
$$1\text{ mcg Vitamin D} = 40\text{ IU}$$
$$1\text{ IU Vitamin D} = 0.025\text{ mcg}$$
- $15\text{ mcg RDA (19–70 y)} = 600\text{ IU}$
- $20\text{ mcg RDA (71+ y)} = 800\text{ IU}$
- $100\text{ mcg UL} = 4,000\text{ IU}$

### C. Vitamin E: $\alpha$-Tocopherol Equivalents
The RDA for vitamin E is based solely on the $2R$-stereoisomeric forms of $\alpha$-tocopherol:
- **Natural $RRR$-$\alpha$-tocopherol** ($d$-$\alpha$-tocopherol):
  $$1\text{ mg } RRR\text{-}\alpha\text{-tocopherol} = 1.49\text{ IU} \iff 1\text{ IU} = 0.67\text{ mg}$$
- **Synthetic $all\text{-}rac$-$\alpha$-tocopherol** ($dl$-$\alpha$-tocopherol):
  $$1\text{ mg } all\text{-}rac\text{-}\alpha\text{-tocopherol} = 1.1\text{ IU} \iff 1\text{ IU} = 0.45\text{ mg (for RDA)}$$
- **Upper Limit**: $1,000\text{ mg}$ applies **strictly to supplemental $\alpha$-tocopherol** to prevent hemorrhagic risk.

### D. Folate: Dietary Folate Equivalents (DFE)
Synthetic folic acid has higher bioavailability than naturally bound food folate:
$$\text{mcg DFE} = \text{mcg food folate} + (1.7 \times \text{mcg synthetic folic acid with food})$$
$$\text{mcg DFE (empty stomach)} = \text{mcg food folate} + (2.0 \times \text{mcg synthetic folic acid})$$
- **Upper Limit**: $1,000\text{ mcg}$ applies **strictly to synthetic folic acid** in supplements and fortified foods (to avoid masking Vitamin B12 deficiency neuropathy). Food folate has no UL.

### E. Niacin: Niacin Equivalents (NE)
Niacin can be synthesized endogenously from the amino acid tryptophan:
$$1\text{ mg NE} = 1\text{ mg preformed niacin (nicotinic acid / nicotinamide)} = 60\text{ mg dietary tryptophan}$$
- **Upper Limit**: $35\text{ mg}$ applies **strictly to synthetic forms** (nicotinic acid/nicotinamide) in supplements and fortified foods to prevent flushing and hepatotoxicity.

### F. Metric Weight Conversions
- $1\text{ g} = 1,000\text{ mg} = 1,000,000\text{ mcg}$
- $1\text{ mg} = 1,000\text{ mcg} = 0.001\text{ g}$
- $1\text{ mcg} = 0.001\text{ mg} = 0.000001\text{ g}$
